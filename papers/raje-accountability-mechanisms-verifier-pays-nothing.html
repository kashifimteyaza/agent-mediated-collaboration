Position: Accountability Mechanisms Are Adopted Only When the Verifier Pays Nothing — Evidence from a Census of Freelance Job Postings

Akshara Raje · Ascendion

Download PDF All accepted papers
§ Abstract

Position paper

Proposals for accountability in agent-mediated work—AI use statements, detection, watermarking, content provenance—presuppose that the parties who would rely on them want them. Whether that demand exists has not been measured in the markets where AI-mediated work is exchanged between strangers. We report a complete enumeration of the active job-posting queues on two freelance platforms, coded against a preregistered scheme using an automated coder validated against hand codes and against injected positive controls. Requests for disclosure of AI use are close to absent, and no posting in either market names watermarking, content credentials, or provenance of any kind. Clients are nevertheless concerned with verification, and those most hostile to AI request it most often. The mechanisms they adopt share a property that the provenance stack does not: each is free to the client, and its cost falls on the applicant. Where the platform sells assurance products directly, uptake is negligible, and several such products are purchased by no client in the corpus. Demand for verification is also close to flat in the value of the contract. Our position is that accountability mechanisms in such markets are selected on cost incidence rather than on design, and that any mechanism imposing a cost on the verifier will not be adopted there. We argue that who pays to verify should be treated as a first-class design constraint, and that demand should be measured rather than assumed.

Keywords: AI disclosure, provenance, watermarking, accountability, freelance work, cost incidence, challenge–response, position paper
