Who Gets to Interpret the User? Processual Contribution and Accountability in EEG-Based AI Body Doubling

Xiangzhe Yuan · Naimul Hoque · Lucas M. Silva · University of Iowa

Download PDF All accepted papers
§ Abstract

Work in progress

Body doubling, the practice of leveraging another person's presence to support focus, accountability, and sustained task engagement, has emerged as a promising productivity strategy, particularly for people with attention regulation difficulties such as ADHD. However, body double companions are not always available, creating an opportunity for systems to provide body doubling support that can adapt in real time to a person's changing cognitive state. We investigate an AI body double system that combines EEG tracking for cognitive state estimation with proactive LLM support to provide adaptive co-regulation during reading, studying, and writing tasks. In a pilot study with three participants, we found that the AI body double as a social presence, an accountability cue, and a source of behavioral guidance, while participants actively evaluated whether its interpretations and interactions were accurate and helpful. These preliminary findings point to AI body doubles as behavioral productivity supports beyond direct contributors to task artifacts. We conceptualize this influence as processual contribution, where AI supports how users carry out their work rather than directly producing task content.

Keywords: Body Doubling, Passive Brain–Computer Interface, Proactive AI, AI Agent, EEG, Cognitive Tracking
