Contribution Dissolution as a Constraint on Realizing the Productivity Gains of AI

Yong Suk Lee · Keough School of Global Affairs, University of Notre Dame, Notre Dame, IN, USA

Download PDF SSRN All accepted papers
§ Abstract

Position paper

Contribution dissolution, the blurring of who did what when work is produced jointly by humans and AI, is often framed as a problem of attribution, originality, and accountability. In this piece I argue that contribution dissolution is also a constraint on organizational productivity in the AI era. Despite consistent experimental evidence of individual-level productivity gains from AI, organizational gains have not materialized. I point to the human verification bottleneck as one mechanism. AI expands generation while the capacity to verify does not, and this bottleneck persists because we lack shared, explicit norms about what may be delegated to AI. Resolving the contribution dissolution problem through normalized delegation conventions, beyond documentation, can help realize the productivity gains from AI.

Keywords: contribution dissolution, AI and productivity, verification bottleneck, delegation norms, organizational economics
