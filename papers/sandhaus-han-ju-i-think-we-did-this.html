§ Position provocation · Nobody Did This, CSCW 2026

“I Think We Did This”: GenAI Contributes to Erosion of Ownership and Design Intention in Group Design Work

Hauke Sandhaus · Ruixiang Han · Wendy Ju · Cornell Tech, New York, NY, USA

Download PDF SSRN All accepted papers
§ Abstract

Work in progress

Increasingly widespread generative AI adoption is changing the nature of group design work. To understand how ownership and design intention shift, we interviewed eighteen students from a graduate interactive device design course where projects are done in teams, each walking through their own repository and chat logs, and we report an emerging finding in three parts. First, design intentions can have histories their authors cannot recover. Final projects narrated as personal ideas traced back to AI brainstorms the students could not place or did not recall. Second, intention forms downstream of the tool. Students discover their preferences by selecting among generated options, ask the model to argue for its own suggestions, and accept its case for scaling an ambition down. Third, the group record dissolves. The model merges teammates’ contributions, its dialogues live in single members’ private accounts, and students turn to design intent cosmetics such as redrawing generated storyboards by hand, work done after the fact that restores a feeling of ownership while obscuring the actual process. We read these accounts through Lessig’s pathetic dot, the designer shaped by an architecture they no longer see. In the worst cases, the designer’s authority over the work erodes, both their intention and their ownership.

Keywords: generative AI, group design work, ownership, attribution, accountability, design education, agent-mediated collaboration
