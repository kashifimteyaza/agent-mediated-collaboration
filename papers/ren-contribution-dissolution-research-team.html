An Account of Contribution Dissolution in a Research Team

Jared Ren · Human Centered Design & Engineering, University of Washington

Download PDF All accepted papers
§ Abstract

Position paper

In spring 2026, our four-person graduate research team spent a quarter interviewing professional visual artists about how they defend authorship when generative AI enters their work. The artists had clear rules about where AI was allowed. We had none, and by the end of the quarter a word none of us could account for had traveled from a coding memo into our final presentation, where our advisor asked why we had chosen it. Nobody could say. This is a first-person account of that collaboration and its quiet breakdowns: coding decisions their own authors could not explain, a suspicion I never voiced, a flagging rule whose silence carried no information, and a disclosure statement that became true only after we had retreated to working manually. I argue that AI does not need to write anyone’s work to dissolve contribution; it only needs to be available in the background of each member’s individual workflow. The conditions that make collaboration accountable live in people, not in records, and no record we kept, or could have kept, would have restored them.

Keywords: contribution dissolution, accountability, generative AI, AI disclosure, collaborative work, qualitative research, autoethnography
