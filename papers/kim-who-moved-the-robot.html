§ Position provocation · Nobody Did This, CSCW 2026

Who Moved the Robot? Contribution Dissolution in Agentic Telepresence

Hyunju Kim · Information Science, Cornell University, Ithaca, NY, USA

Download PDF All accepted papers
§ Abstract

Position paper

Agentic telepresence systems increasingly generate movements and gestures on behalf of remote users. This automation reduces operational burden, but it also makes it difficult to establish whether the remote user retains meaningful authority over the judgments a robot’s action expresses. A robot’s behavior may be attributed to the remote user even when it is jointly produced by a local collaborator, the remote user, and an autonomous system, and even when the remote user did not meaningfully shape the judgment it communicates. Drawing on prior work and ongoing research in telepresence, I describe two forms of embodied contribution dissolution: temporal dissolution, in which control moves among actors over time, and structural dissolution, in which several actors jointly constitute one action from the outset. In both cases, a person may remain accountable for judgments they can neither recognize, explain, nor defend as their own. I close by asking what collaborative systems must preserve for such accountability to remain grounded in recognizable and defensible human judgment, and whether these conditions differ between textual and embodied collaboration.

Keywords: agentic telepresence, contribution dissolution, embodied collaboration, accountability, shared control
